# Practitioner pilot protocol

**Status: prepared, not conducted.** No independent journalist, environmental researcher or NGO worker has tested this prototype. Automated browser tests and the builder's own review do not count as practitioner validation. Do not claim adoption, investigation-time savings or emissions reduction from this protocol.

## When a participant becomes available

Invite one person who regularly examines environmental claims. Ask for permission to record anonymous task observations and quotations; do not record their identity, private reports or unpublished investigations without explicit permission. Participation and quotation are optional. No payment, account or wallet is needed.

Give them the public application URL without explaining the interface. Say: “Use this tool to check a methane statement and share your finding with another reader.” Observe first; help only when they ask. Record every intervention.

## Tasks

1. Find source `AZE_S_010`. Use the explicitly labelled practice statement for 1 May 2026 through 1 June inclusive. Explain which dates are included.
2. Run the evidence check. Identify the number of matching observations, sensors and dates. Explain what the records establish and what they do not establish about physical repair.
3. Record a decision and a corrected statement in their own words. Save it. Reload the page and find the review again.
4. Download the JSON packet and readable report. Import the JSON in Verify and explain the separate file-integrity and source-completeness results.
5. In the optional omission exercise, remove a record and rehash. Explain why the hash can match while the source check fails.
6. Choose a date interval without matching detections. Explain why this cannot establish zero emissions or continuous satellite coverage.

Expected reference answers: the practice interval contains three observations from two sensors (EnMAP and EMIT), dated 26 May, 28 May and 1 June. The end boundary is 2 June, excluded. Physical mitigation is not assessed. No external public blockchain commitment is established by the example.

## Observation sheet

For each task record: completed independently / completed with help / not completed; elapsed time; wrong turn; exact help given; participant's explanation; severity of any misunderstanding. Record device/browser and network conditions. Timing begins after reading each task and ends when the participant says they are finished; keep loading delays and assistance visible. An N=1 result is a case study, not a population estimate.

Ask afterwards:

- What did you expect AfterPlume to do?
- Which label or step was unclear?
- What would you need before using this in an actual investigation?
- Did any result sound more certain than the evidence justified?
- Would the export let another reader reproduce your decision? What is missing?

## Reporting

Keep raw anonymized observations and task failures. Report participant count, role category, test conditions and assistance. Separate observed task completion from self-reported usefulness. Publish a quotation only with permission. Compare before/after task times only with a defined baseline, equivalent tasks and an explicit study design; do not convert automated test durations into human time saved.
